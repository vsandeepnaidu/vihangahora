Reference

Common questions

Short answers to the things people ask first.

Is Pañcapakṣī a kind of astrology?

It belongs to the same family of Indian time-keeping, but it works differently from a horoscope. There are no planets to place and no chart to read. It uses four things from the calendar (the Moon’s star at your birth, the half of the lunar month, the weekday and sunrise) and a set of fixed tables.

Do I need my exact time of birth?

Usually not. Your bird depends on the Moon’s star and on the half of the lunar month, and the Moon stays in one star for roughly a day. The date is enough for most people. Time and place matter only if the star or the half changed on the day you were born; in that case there are two possible birds, and the birth time decides between them.

I was born close to New Moon or Full Moon. Which half do I use?

This is the one place where a little care is needed, because the half reverses the star table. Find the exact time the half changed on your birth date from an almanac and compare it with your birth time. If you cannot settle it, read both birds for a few weeks and see which one fits your experience.

Another book or app gives me a different bird. Why?

The most common cause is a misprint in one well-known table, which gives the wrong bird for the last six stars (Shravana to Revati). The second is the half of the month being taken differently near New or Full Moon. For name birds, the dark-half list also varies between sources. The Variants and sources page lists every such point.

Another source gives different times for the same day. Why?

Almost always because of sunrise. Some tables fix sunrise at 6:00 AM everywhere; others use the real sunrise for your place, which stretches or shrinks every slot. Both are traditional. On the Today page you can switch between them and see the difference.

Does my bird ever change?

A bird found from your birth star is for life. A bird found from the sound of a name changes every fortnight, when the Moon’s half changes.

Is a death day dangerous?

No. It is the traditional name for the weekday on which a bird is weakest. Nothing is predicted for the person. The practical advice is modest: keep the day for routine, and choose another day for something important if you have the choice.

Do I have to plan my whole day around this?

No, and nobody does. The method is most useful for the few things where you are free to choose the time. For those, pick a good turn. Everything else carries on as usual.

Does it work outside India, or in the southern hemisphere?

Yes. The method follows the local sunrise, sunset and weekday, wherever you are. Far from the equator the day and night slots become very unequal in summer and winter, so real sunrise matters more there. In polar regions, on dates when the Sun does not rise or set, the division into day and night slots has nothing to rest on, and people fall back on the 6-to-6 reckoning.

What about daylight saving time?

The method follows the Sun, not the clock. If you use real sunrise, nothing changes: the clock times of your slots simply move by an hour along with sunrise. If you use fixed 6-to-6 times, they will be an hour out against the Sun while daylight saving is in force.

How long is a slot, and how long is a turn?

A slot is one fifth of the day or of the night: 2 hours 24 minutes when sunrise and sunset are twelve hours apart. A turn is between one twelfth and one third of a slot, which is 12 to 48 minutes at that length.

Two of us have the same bird. Do we read the day the same way?

Yes. The bird is all that the tables ask about a person, so two people with the same bird in the same place have the same day.

Where does the data on this site come from?

From one rule file, the same one the Vihanga Hora app uses. It was compiled from the classical sources and checked against several of them. Every table on the site is generated from it, so the site and the app cannot drift apart.

Does this site collect anything about me?

No. There are no accounts and no tracking. The bird you pick, your sunrise setting and your lesson progress are kept in your own browser so the pages remember them. If you use “My location” on the Today page, the position is used on your device to work out sunrise and is not sent anywhere. The page fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.
