Lesson 7 of 14

Turns: the finer rhythm

Five unequal parts inside each slot, and the bird that shares them.

A slot of nearly two and a half hours is a blunt instrument. The tradition sharpens it by dividing each slot into five turns of unequal length.

How a slot divides

  • The first turn is always the slot’s own main activity.
  • The other four follow in the same order the slots use (lesson 6), wrapping round.
  • Each activity has a set length, and that length is different by day and by night and in each half.

For a bright-half day the lengths are:

Eating 30 min → Walking 36 min → Ruling 48 min → Sleeping 18 min → Dying 12 min

They add up to 144 minutes, one 6-to-6 slot.

With real sunrise the lengths keep their proportions and stretch or shrink with the slot. A turn that is 48 minutes in a 144-minute slot is one third of the slot whatever its real length.

The sharing bird

The last column is new. During each turn, your bird is said to share the time with another bird: the one whose main activity in this slot is the same as your turn activity.

In the example, while the Peacock is in its Sleeping turn, it shares with whichever bird has Sleeping as its main activity for the slot. The first turn always matches your own main activity, so it is shared with nobody: it is your own turn.

The sharing bird matters because birds have friends and enemies (lesson 9). A good turn shared with a friend stays good; shared with an enemy it loses some of its shine.

In short. Slot gives the main activity; turn gives the activity of the moment. Read both: “a Ruling slot, Eating turn”. Each turn after the first is shared with another bird.
