Lesson 5 of 14

How a day is laid out

The half of the month, the weekday from sunrise, and ten slots.

Before any table can be read, four questions have to be answered about the moment you care about. All four have a small trap in them.

1 · Which half of the month?

The lunar month has two halves. The bright half (வளர்பிறை) runs from just after New Moon up to Full Moon. The dark half (தேய்பிறை) runs from just after Full Moon down to New Moon. Each lasts about a fortnight.

Everything in Panchapakshi comes in two versions, one for each half: the order of activities, the lengths of the turns, the ruling days, the death days, even which birds are friends. Using the wrong half is the commonest way to get a wrong answer.

On the days the half changes, sources differ on where exactly the line falls. A common rule is to take the half that holds at sunrise for the whole day. Check an almanac near New and Full Moon. The live tool on this site estimates the half and warns you when a change is close.

2 · Which weekday?

A Panchapakshi day starts at sunrise, not at midnight. It runs through the daytime, through the following night, and ends at the next sunrise.

So at two in the morning on what the calendar calls Tuesday, it is still Monday night. You read Monday’s night table until the Sun comes up.

3 · Day or night?

Day is sunrise to sunset; night is sunset to the next sunrise. The two have separate tables.

There are two ways to set the clock:

  • 6-to-6 reckoning. Treat sunrise as 6:00 AM and sunset as 6:00 PM everywhere. Simple, and close enough near the equator.
  • Real sunrise and sunset for your place and date. More faithful to the method, and it matters more the further you are from the equator and the closer to midsummer or midwinter, when the day and night are far from equal.

4 · Which slot?

The day is cut into five equal slots, and the night into five more. With the 6-to-6 reckoning each slot is exactly 2 hours 24 minutes (144 minutes). With real sunrise, a day slot is one fifth of the actual daylight and a night slot one fifth of the actual night, so in summer the day slots are long and the night slots short.

In each slot your bird has one main activity. Lesson 6 shows where it comes from.

In short. Fix the half of the month, the weekday (from sunrise), day or night, and the slot. Those four facts plus your bird are all the tables ever ask for.
