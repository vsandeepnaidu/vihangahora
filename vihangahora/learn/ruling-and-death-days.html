Lesson 8 of 14

Ruling days and death days

The days that lift a bird, and the days that mute it.

So far every weekday has looked alike in weight. It is not. For each bird, some weekdays lift the whole day and others lower it.

Ruling days and nights

The bird that is Eating in the first slot rules that day, and the bird Eating in the first slot of the night rules that night. On a day or night your bird rules, tradition counts its favourable turns as stronger than usual.

Death days

Each weekday is also the death day of one bird. A death day covers the full Panchapakshi day, sunrise to sunrise, day and night together.

A death day outranks everything inside it. Even a Ruling slot is treated as having little force. The tradition’s advice is plain: keep such a day for routine, and move anything important to another day if you can.

The two halves have different lists, so a bird’s good and difficult weekdays switch roughly every fortnight.

When both fall together

In the dark half a few birds rule a night that sits inside their own death day. The sources treat that night as neutral: the two cancel, and it is read as an ordinary night.

Order of reading

When you assess a moment, look at the day first and the clock second:

  1. Is today a death day for my bird? If so, the day is muted whatever the slots say.
  2. Does my bird rule this day or night? If so, good turns count for more.
  3. Otherwise it is an ordinary day, and the slot and turn decide.

In short. One bird rules each day and each night; one bird has each weekday as its death day. Check the day before the hour. The lists change with the half of the month.

Check yourself

Your bird is in a Ruling slot, but today is its death day. How is the slot read?
