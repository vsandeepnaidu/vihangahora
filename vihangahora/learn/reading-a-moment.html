Lesson 10 of 14

Reading a moment

Putting slot, turn, sharer and day together into one grade.

You now have every piece. A moment is described by a slot, a turn, the bird that shares the turn, and the kind of day it falls on. The tradition boils these down to a simple grade.

One moment, read in full

Here is the Peacock at 9:50 AM on the bright-half Friday used in earlier lessons, shown the way the live card on this site and the Vihanga Hora app show it.

  1. The headline is the slot. It gives the bird’s main activity and the time the slot ends (lesson 6).
  2. The strip is the slot’s five turns, drawn to length and coloured by activity. The marker shows where the moment falls (lesson 7).
  3. The line under it names the turn and how long it has left.
  4. Then the sharing bird, and whether it is a friend or an enemy (lessons 7 and 9).
  5. Last, the grade, which the rules below produce from the four items above.

In this example the slot and the turn are both favourable, yet the grade is only medium. The reason is the enemy sharing the turn. The rules explain why.

The grading rules

  1. A Sleeping or Dying turn is bad, whatever slot it is in.
  2. A Walking turn is medium.
  3. A Ruling or Eating turn inside a Sleeping or Dying slot is medium. The texts call it a faint or weak good: the turn is favourable but the slot under it is not.
  4. A Ruling or Eating turn inside a Ruling, Eating or Walking slot is good, unless the bird sharing it is an enemy, in which case it drops to medium.

Among the good combinations, the texts rank a Ruling turn in a Ruling slot highest, then the mixes of Ruling and Eating, then Eating in Eating.

Then apply the day

  • On a ruling day or night, the good turns are at their best.
  • On a death day, even good turns are treated as weak.
  • On an ordinary day the grade stands as it is.

What the grades are for

Good, medium and bad are the tradition’s labels, and it helps to translate them into plans rather than verdicts:

  • Good: the time to begin, decide, ask or present.
  • Medium: ordinary time. Carry on with what is already under way.
  • Bad: a pause. Prepare, tidy up, rest. Most of these turns are over within half an hour.

Nobody arranges a whole life around turns. The practical use is narrow and realistic: when you have a choice about when to do something that matters to you, choose a good turn.

The live Today page applies exactly these rules to the current moment and to every turn of the day.

In short. Bad if the turn is Sleeping or Dying. Medium if it is Walking, or if the slot beneath is Sleeping or Dying, or if an enemy shares it. Otherwise good. Then let the day lift or mute the result.
