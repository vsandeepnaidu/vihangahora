Lesson 3 of 14

The five activities

Ruling, Eating, Walking, Sleeping and Dying: a ladder of strength.

Each bird is always doing one of five things. The Tamil names are short and concrete, and the English ones translate them directly.

Ruling

அரசு · strength 1

The bird is at full strength and in command. Tradition treats this as the time for the thing that matters most.

Eating

ஊண் · strength 0.8

The bird is feeding and gaining. A steady, productive state, second only to Ruling.

Walking

நடை · strength 0.6

The bird is on the move. Neither strong nor weak: fine for ordinary work, average for anything important.

Sleeping

துயில் · strength 0.4

The bird is at rest. Low energy; better suited to pausing, reviewing and preparing.

Dying

சாவு · strength 0.2

The bird is spent. The weakest state of the five. Tradition says to wait and not to begin anything new.

The strength figures are the ones the texts use when two birds are compared: 1.0, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4 and 0.2. Some Tamil handbooks give the same ladder as 100, 80, 50, 25 and 0 per cent.

Names that sound stronger than they are

“Dying” and “death day” are the traditional terms and this site keeps them, but they describe the bird’s strength, not anything that happens to a person. A Dying turn is simply the low point of a cycle that comes round several times a day and passes within the hour.

Five birds, five different activities

At any moment no two birds are doing the same thing. If the Crow is Ruling, the other four hold the remaining four activities between them. This is the rule that makes the whole system fit together, and it is also a quick way to check a table: every column must contain each activity exactly once.

A first rule of thumb

  • Ruling and Eating are the times the tradition favours for starting and deciding.
  • Walking is in between. Treat it as ordinary time.
  • Sleeping and Dying are for holding back.

The lessons that follow refine this, because a moment has two layers (a slot and a turn inside it) and the day itself can lift or lower everything. But this three-line version is already most of the practice.

In short. Five activities on a ladder from Ruling down to Dying. The five birds never share one. Favour the top two, treat the middle as neutral, wait out the bottom two.

Check yourself
