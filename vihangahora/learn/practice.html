Lesson 14 of 14

Using it well

Daily and weekly rhythm, common slips, and a month of practice.

Knowing the rules is the easy half. This last lesson is about fitting them into an actual life without letting them run it.

A daily rhythm

One way to use the five activities as a planner:

When your bird isA sensible use of the time
RulingThe one thing that matters most today: a decision, a first step, a conversation you have been putting off.
EatingDeep, steady work. Study, building, writing, anything that rewards concentration.
WalkingThe ordinary run of the day: errands, messages, follow-ups, travel that is already arranged.
SleepingA lower gear. Reviewing, planning, a walk, a meal.
DyingA short pause. Clear the desk, finish small things, and start nothing new until it passes.

A turn lasts between twelve and forty-eight minutes, which happens to be a good length for a single stretch of focused work. Some people time their work sessions to the length of the current turn.

A weekly rhythm

  • At the start of the week, note which days your bird rules and which is its death day. Put what matters on the first and keep the second light.
  • Check where the Moon is. Two or three times a month the half changes, and with it every table you are using.

With other people

  • If you can propose the time for a meeting or a request, propose one inside a Ruling or Eating turn of your own.
  • For something shared, look for a time that is at least neutral for everyone involved.

Six slips that explain most wrong readings

  1. Midnight is not the boundary. Until sunrise it is still the previous weekday’s night.
  2. The wrong half. Bright-half tables in the dark half, or the reverse, especially on the changeover days.
  3. A fixed name bird. Name birds switch with the half. Only a star bird stays.
  4. Trusting a Ruling slot on a death day. The day outranks the slot.
  5. Spelling instead of sound. A name bird comes from the first vowel you hear.
  6. Stopping at the slot. The turn can make a good slot a poor moment, and a poor slot a fair one.

A seventh, for anyone far from the equator: fixed 6 AM times can be more than an hour out in summer and winter. Use real sunrise.

Four weeks of practice

  • Week 1 · Names. Find your bird and the birds of the people close to you. Learn the five birds and the five activities until you can say them in order without thinking.
  • Week 2 · Slots. Each morning, write down your bird’s ten main activities for the day. In the evening, note in a line how the day actually went.
  • Week 3 · Turns. Add the turns for your Ruling and Eating slots, and do one deliberate thing each day inside a good turn.
  • Week 4 · People. Bring in friends, enemies and one other person’s bird. Plan one conversation with the method.

Keep the notes. A month of your own one-line records, read back, will teach you more about whether and how this works for you than any table.

Using it rightly

The old teachers were firm that this knowledge is for acting well, not for getting the better of others. In the same spirit:

  • Treat timing as one consideration among many. Preparation, skill and other people’s needs still count for more.
  • Never let a “bad” turn be a reason to put off something that cannot wait.
  • Do not use it to worry anyone, yourself included, about their days.

That is the whole method. From here, the bird pages and tables are for looking things up, and the Today page does the arithmetic for you.
