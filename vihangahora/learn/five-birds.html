The five birds

Vulture, Owl, Crow, Cock and Peacock, and what tradition links to each.

The five birds always appear in the same order: Vulture, Owl, Crow, Cock, Peacock. The order matters, because many tables simply walk along it.

Each bird is tied to one of the five short vowels of Tamil, in alphabetical order: அ இ உ எ ஒ (a, i, u, e, o). That link is the basis of finding a bird from a name, which lesson 4 explains.

One bird per person

You do not choose a bird and it does not describe your character. It is an address: it tells you which row of the tables is yours. Two people with the same bird read the same day in the same way.

No bird is better than another. Over a week every bird rules, eats, walks, sleeps and dies in its turn. The birds differ only in when each of those comes round.

Traditional associations

The sources also attach each bird to a letter of the five-syllable mantra, an element, a direction and a colour. Most of these change between the bright and the dark half of the month. They are part of the tradition’s picture of the birds and are not needed to read a day.

The sources do not fully agree on these. Colours and elements in particular have more than one version; see Variants and sources.

In short. Five birds in a fixed order, one per person. Your bird decides which line of each table you read, nothing more.
