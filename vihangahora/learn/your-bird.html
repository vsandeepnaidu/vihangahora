Lesson 4 of 14

Finding your bird

From your birth star, or from the first sound of a name.

There are two ways to find a bird. The first uses the Moon at your birth and gives a bird for life. The second uses the sound of your name and is the fallback when birth details are unknown.

Method 1 · The birth star

Indian astronomy divides the Moon’s monthly path into 27 equal stretches called stars (nakshatras). The star the Moon was in when you were born is your birth star. You also need to know whether you were born in the bright half of the lunar month (Moon growing) or the dark half (Moon shrinking).

The 27 stars fall into five groups, one for each bird. In the bright half the groups run in the usual bird order. In the dark half the order is reversed.

Star groups and their birds
StarsFrom – toBorn in the bright halfBorn in the dark half
1 – 5Ashwini to MrigashiraVulturePeacock
6 – 11Ardra to Purva PhalguniOwlCock
12 – 16Uttara Phalguni to VishakhaCrowCrow
17 – 21Anuradha to Uttara AshadhaCockOwl
22 – 27Shravana to RevatiPeacockVulture

The middle group gives the Crow in both halves, because the middle of a list stays put when the list is reversed.

If you do not know your star. Any Indian almanac (panchangam) gives the star and the half for a date, time and place. The Vihanga Hora app works both out from your birth date, time and city on your own device.

A bird found this way never changes. This is the bird to use whenever birth details are known.

Method 2 · The sound of the name

Take the name a person is actually called by, and listen for its first vowel sound. Each of the five vowels belongs to a bird. A long vowel counts with its short one, and ai and au count with a.

Three things to keep in mind with name birds:

  • It is the sound, not the spelling. In English spelling the first vowel letter can mislead. “Jacob” starts with the sound ee, “Uma” with u, “Irfan” with i. A consonant simply carries its vowel: “Ravi” is a, “Leela” is ii.
  • A name bird changes every fortnight. The same name gives one bird in the bright half and another in the dark half. A star bird does not do this.
  • Use it when you must. It is the traditional way to place someone whose birth details you do not have, such as the other person in a meeting.

The dark-half column of the name table is one of the places where printed sources disagree. The table above shows the reading the app currently follows. See Variants and sources.

A third bird you may meet

Some texts also give a bird to each rising sign of the zodiac. It belongs to the question-answering side of the tradition and is not used for reading a day, so this course leaves it aside.

In short. Birth star plus the half of the month gives a bird for life. The first vowel sound of a name gives a bird that switches with the half. Prefer the first; use the second for people whose birth details you lack.
